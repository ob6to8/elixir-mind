# Understanding a decision is not standing to judge it

The belief, as stated:

> An operator who outsourced judgment while a system was designed cannot audit
> it merely by coming to understand it. The standing to judge which of its
> complexity is incidental and which is load-bearing was outsourced along with
> the judgment, and must be reconstructed separately — by re-making decisions,
> not by re-reading them.

The source phrase, from the operator's
[2026-07-31 journal entry](/journal/2026-07-31.md):

> "the challenge of wading through this repo and reconciling it to match my own
> mental model — as well as granting me the authority to do so, given that I've
> outsourced a certain degree of my critical faculties to the agent in
> developing and designing this"

**Why it is harder than comprehension debt.** Comprehension debt is repaid by
study: read the system until you can predict its behavior. This belief holds
that a second debt survives that repayment — you can understand a decision and
still not know whether it was justified. Justification lives in the
alternatives weighed and the constraints in force at decision time, which is
exactly what was delegated; the committed artifact records the choice, not the
choosing. So audit authority is reconstructed by **re-deciding**: re-deriving
the alternatives and checking the committed choice against them, as if the
decision were being made fresh. Where the choosing was persisted — a plan's
decision list, an analysis's weighed verdict — the reconstruction is cheap and
the persistence is why. Where the rationale lived only in an agent's context,
re-reading the result cannot recover it, and the operator re-decides from
scratch or accepts the choice on trust.

**Why it is a belief and not a claim.** It is a normative statement about the
conditions of legitimate judgment — what *entitles* an auditor to a verdict —
and that is not something this bundle could settle empirically. It is held true
enough to act on: it says an audit that produces only understanding has not
finished, and puts the burden of proof on the ratify-what-you-now-comprehend
shortcut.

**What it rules out.** Treating a comprehension audit as sufficient to ratify
existing architecture. Understanding-first review is necessary — a design not
understood cannot be judged at all — but a design understood is not yet a
design endorsed, and an audit that stops at "I now see what this does" has
converted outsourced judgment into rubber-stamped judgment with extra steps.

**Where it is acted on.** The comprehension-and-auditing transition committed
in the [2026-07-28 journal entry](/journal/2026-07-28.md) — this belief is the
reason that audit must weigh decisions and not only trace them. It is also the
demand-side argument for the artifacts that persist choosing:
[persist-plans](/meta/policy/persist-plans.md) and the decision lists of
[structured-plan-bodies](/meta/policy/structured-plan-bodies.md) are what make
authority reconstruction affordable, because they keep the alternatives on the
record instead of in a reclaimed session context.

**Its limit.** It does not demand re-deciding everything — the reconstruction
effort is proportional to how load-bearing the decision is, and most
implementation detail warrants trust once its layer's contract is judged. And
it carries a recursion: the persisted rationale is itself largely
agent-authored, so reconstructing authority *from* it still leans on the
outsourced faculty. The recursion bottoms out only where the operator re-makes
the decision in their own judgment; the artifacts shorten that path but cannot
substitute for it.
