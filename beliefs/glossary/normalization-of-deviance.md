# normalization of deviance

Coined in Diane Vaughan's analysis of the Space Shuttle *Challenger* disaster:
engineers and managers repeatedly flew with a known O-ring defect because
each successful flight was treated as evidence the risk was acceptable,
rather than as one more unearned pass. The mechanism generalizes past
aerospace to any system where a near-miss's absence of visible consequence
gets misread as validation instead of luck — the guardrail erodes a little
further each time nothing goes wrong. Applied to agentic AI, the term names
organizations skipping human oversight or shipping known-risky agent
capabilities because "it worked last time," confusing the absence of a
successful attack with the presence of robust security.
