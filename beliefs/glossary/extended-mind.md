# extended mind

Andy Clark and David Chalmers introduced the position in 1998, arguing it from
the [parity principle](/beliefs/glossary/parity-principle.md) and illustrating it
with Otto, an Alzheimer's patient whose notebook occupies the functional role a
biological memory would. Three conditions carry the weight: the resource is
constantly available, retrieval is easy, and what is retrieved is endorsed
without re-checking.

Read as a specification rather than a philosophical position, those conditions
are what a personal knowledge system must engineer — and the third is the
expensive one, since endorsement without re-checking is rational only where
something guarantees the store's integrity. That reading is the subject of
[second mind as extended cognition with an executor](/knowledge/knowledge-management/extended-cognition/second-mind-as-extended-cognition-with-an-executor.md).
