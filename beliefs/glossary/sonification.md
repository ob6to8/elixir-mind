# sonification

What makes it *systematic* is the mapping: a defensible sonification binds every
audible parameter — pitch, loudness, density, timbre, position — to a variable,
so a listener can decode the sound back into the data. Sound added for
atmosphere breaks that contract and turns the display into a soundtrack.

The field distinguishes several mapping strategies:
**parameter-mapping** (a data dimension drives a synthesis parameter, the most
common form), **audification** (a data series is played directly as a waveform,
used where the data is already signal-like), **model-based** sonification (the
listener excites a virtual object whose behavior encodes the data), and discrete
event markers — see [earcon](/beliefs/glossary/earcon.md) and
[auditory icon](/beliefs/glossary/auditory-icon.md).

Its structural advantage over a visual display is that time is sound's native
axis rather than a borrowed one, so rate, concurrency, and change need no
playhead, no animation, and no gaze direction. Its structural weakness is
absolute precision: a listener reads relations and change accurately and reads
*values* badly, which is why sonification usually augments a visual display
rather than replacing it. The brain's own uses are as a
[perceptual gate for temporal data](/meta/analysis/media-production-domain-synergies.md),
as a swarm-pathology monitor in the
[declared-cadence analysis](/meta/analysis/declared-cadence-swarm-auditability.md),
and as the relief channel in the
[incident-replay analysis](/meta/analysis/sonifying-an-incident-replay.md).
