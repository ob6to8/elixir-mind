# Governance prose

The tier assignment rests on a specific property: such an edit **fails
visibly**. A wrong sentence in a skill body or an index gloss is apparent to the
next reader at the cost of reading it, and correcting it costs one more edit —
which is what actually separates the delegable tiers from the ones reserved for
judgment, more reliably than "is there an oracle" does.

The category exists because these edits fit none of
[the roster's](/meta/model-roster.md) four original rows. Not a canonical body,
no open decision, not bulk — and no oracle checks the content, so even the
Sonnet row's own qualifier ("gated by `mix brain.verify`/the suite") did not
literally apply. Two independent scoping runs reported the gap in their own
words before the row existed, each straining to justify a stamp the roster gave
them no clean basis for.

**Two boundaries make the category decidable rather than merely present:**

- **Policies and doctrine are excluded.** They are canonical bodies that
  compile into the contract, and they stay at the top tier however small the
  edit. The distinction is what the text *is for* — a policy establishes the
  rule others derive from; a skill body tells an agent how to carry it out.
- **A fresh decision is not prose.** If the edit's content is a decision being
  made rather than one being rendered, it is stamped by the decision's weight.
  Writing a policy that states an already-ratified rule is governance prose;
  choosing what the rule should say is a taxonomy call. The same edit can be
  either, and which one it is depends on what the packet leaves open.

Coined to close one recurring collision in the stamping procedure, not the
family of them — the general tie-break across the roster's four questions
remains the "hardest motion" metaphor, and the residual is tracked in
[the session-dependent determination issue](/meta/issues/model-determination-is-session-dependent.md).
