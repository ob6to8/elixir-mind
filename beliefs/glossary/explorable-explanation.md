# explorable explanation

Coined by Bret Victor for the genre of interactive documents that hand the
reader a live control over a model's variables instead of arguing a claim in
prose — the reader confirms or breaks the claim themselves, by moving a
slider or dragging a point and watching the result change continuously.
Jack Schaedler's *Circles, Sines, and Signals*, which teaches the Discrete
Fourier Transform this way, sits squarely in the genre.

**In this brain:** the mechanism behind that resource's teaching is distilled
into the [explorable-explanations methodology](/knowledge/knowledge-management/technical-communication/explorable-explanations.md)
(`em:e12137`) — state an equation once, then hand over a control over the
one variable that reveals its actual claim, reusing one visual metaphor
across a sequence and making a wrong prediction falsifiable within one
manipulation. Demonstrated by building
[a five-widget page](/knowledge/machine-learning/evolutionary-computation/evolutionary-search-in-latent-space.md)
(`em:70f026`) over an unrelated paper's equations, filed under the new
[`visualization` type](/beliefs/glossary/visualization-type.md).
