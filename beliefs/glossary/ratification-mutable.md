# ratification-mutable

The property sits deliberately between two failure modes: a truly *immutable*
baseline cannot learn (a growing system must be able to change its own
rules), while a freely *mutable* one cannot resist
[scar tissue](/beliefs/glossary/scar-tissue.md) (local fixes silently
promoting themselves into standing behavior). A ratification-mutable artifact
resolves the tension procedurally: **what distinguishes learning from drift
is who approved the change**, not whether the change looked locally rational.
The operator's equivalent formulation, *ratification-immutable* — immutable
except by ratification — names the same property from the guarantee side
(what agents can rely on) rather than the capability side (how the operator
evolves it); both denote one channel, one approver.

**In this brain:** the property of the [operating contract](/CLAUDE.md) and
everything compiled into it — the type vocabulary, the top-level taxonomy,
the attribution channel list — per the
[taxonomy-evolution protocol](/meta/policy/taxonomy-evolution-protocol.md).
Contrast the [attribution](/beliefs/glossary/attribution.md) event sub-keys,
which are *plainly* immutable (written once, no channel changes them, not
even ratification — history is not up for approval), and ordinary bundle
documents, which are plainly mutable (agents update in place autonomously).
The three regimes — immutable history, ratification-mutable law, mutable
content — are the bundle's adaptation-governance gradient, and the middle one
is what the [bound-adaptation doctrine](/meta/doctrine/bound-adaptation.md)
names as its amendment to the "immutable behavioral contract" of its source
quote.
