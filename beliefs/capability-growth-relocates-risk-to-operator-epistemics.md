# Capability growth relocates agentic risk from model behavior to operator epistemics

The belief, as stated:

> As models grow more capable, the operative risk of working with them
> relocates: away from what the model might do, and into what the operator
> comes to falsely believe. Capability invites stripping guidance and
> verification; the fraction of work that is wrong anyway — assumed correct
> because the model is trusted — compounds through the artifacts into a
> misaligned mental model of the system the operator is nominally directing.

The source phrases, from the operator's
[2026-07-31 journal entry](/journal/2026-07-31.md):

> "as models get more capable, the temptation to strip guidance and enforcement
> increases"

> "it looks less like Skynet and more like false assumptions and misaligned
> mental models by the operator"

**Its grounding is three days of journal converging.** The
[2026-07-26 entry](/journal/2026-07-26.md) catalogued the feared risks that
kept failing to materialize — internet-connected takeover, AGI eschatology,
monopoly capture — leaving open where the realized risk actually lives. The
[2026-07-29 entry](/journal/2026-07-29.md) supplied the mechanism on the human
side: effortless delivery degrades the operator's own faculties. The 2026-07-31
entry closes the triangle with a lived instance — the music-tour advancing
system, whose quiet misses compounded into a falsely recorded logistical state
that collided with reality at human cost, and whose verification tax ended up
doubling the work. In each case the damage traveled through the operator's
picture of the system, and the model's behavior was only ever the first link.

**Why it is a belief and not a claim.** It is a value-laden prior about where
danger lives — a bet placed against the prevailing trust-the-model framing, not
a measurement. No experiment this bundle could run would settle "the operative
risk" across systems and operators; the statement is held true enough to shape
design, which is exactly the work it already does here.

**What it rules out.** Measuring agentic safety at the model layer alone, and
treating capability gains as reducing the need for verification. Under this
belief the opposite holds: the more the model can be trusted with, the larger
the unexamined surface on which the operator's assumptions can silently
diverge — so verification infrastructure becomes more valuable with capability,
not less. Natural-language steering may be consumed by capability; the checks
that bind the artifact were never compensating for capability and are not
released by it.

**Where it is acted on.** The gate suite and `mix brain.verify` (verification
priced up front rather than paid at collision with reality); the journal's own
charter — the operator, not the agent, does the synthesis; and the
comprehension audit committed in the
[2026-07-28 entry](/journal/2026-07-28.md), which treats the operator's mental
model, not the agent's output, as the asset under repair.

**Its limit.** It does not deny model-layer risk exists or that alignment work
matters; it locates the *operative* risk for the class of system this brain
belongs to — a single operator directing agents that produce durable
artifacts. In settings where model outputs act on the world without an
operator's mental model in the loop, the relocation this belief names has
nowhere to land, and the model layer is again where the risk sits.
