# technical-communication

How technical ideas move between people: exposition methods, elicitation
methods, tone, and the structure of explanations — distinct from
[argumentation](/knowledge/knowledge-management/argumentation/index.md)
(the structure of *justification*); this directory is about the structure of
*teaching, presenting, and drawing out*.

## Contents

- [Escalating-example exposition](/knowledge/knowledge-management/technical-communication/escalating-example-exposition.md) — a presentation playbook distilled from Yos Riady's ECS article: open inside one concrete scenario, escalate the same memorable example until the design visibly breaks, name the problem only after it is felt, then rebuild with the alternative using the same cast.
- [The phantom sketch artist method](/knowledge/knowledge-management/technical-communication/phantom-sketch-artist-method.md) — an elicitation playbook: knowing and expressing are different skills, so draw the system back to the person who holds it and let them correct the drawing, iterating from "that's wrong" to recognition.
- [Explorable explanations](/knowledge/knowledge-management/technical-communication/explorable-explanations.md) — a presentation playbook distilled from Circles, Sines, and Signals: state an equation once, then hand the reader a live control over one of its variables so the claim is falsified or confirmed by direct manipulation, not argued in prose.
- [Circles, Sines, and Signals](/knowledge/knowledge-management/technical-communication/circles-sines-signals.md) — Jack Schaedler's interactive, draggable-widget introduction to the Discrete Fourier Transform; the exemplar behind the explorable-explanations method above.
