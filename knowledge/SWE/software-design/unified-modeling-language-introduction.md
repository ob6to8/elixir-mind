# Unified Modeling Language (UML)

UML is "a general-purpose modeling language" — a standardized visual notation
for documenting software system design, not a programming language. It was
approved as an ISO standard in 2005, giving teams a common notation to
communicate architecture and structure across projects, including with
non-technical stakeholders, before implementation begins.

## Purpose

UML lets stakeholders visualize and discuss a design's shape ahead of time,
making complex requirements legible to business participants and catching
structural mismatches while they're still cheap to fix.

## The two diagram categories

- **Structural diagrams** — the static elements of a system and their
  relationships: Class, Composite Structure, Object, Component, Deployment,
  Package.
- **Behavioral diagrams** — how those elements interact over time and
  respond to events: State Machine, Activity, Use Case, Sequence,
  Communication, Interaction Overview.

UML 2.x expanded the notation from 9 to 13 diagram types, extending it to
cover concepts more central to agile and microservices-style architectures
than the original set anticipated.
