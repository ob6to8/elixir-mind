# Reflections on a Decade of Coding

Jamie Brandon (scattered-thoughts.net) reflects on ten years of professional
programming, arguing his growth came from **accumulated changes in habits,
process, and values** rather than any innate ability — and that the specific
changes which made him more effective are largely absent from, or actively
contradict, commonly circulated programming advice.

## Evidence cited for growth

- Built a SQL query planner that passes over six million generated test cases.
- Independently rediscovered a working approach to SQL subquery
  decorrelation.
- Identified consistency failure modes in streaming systems.
- Built a personal text editor from scratch.

He frames these not as marks of exceptional talent but as what ten years of
deliberate, specialized practice should produce in anyone.

## The claim about advice

The essay's central complaint is a **gap in the literature**: material aimed
at beginners is abundant, but writing that helps an already-competent
programmer get measurably better is scarce — and what does circulate as
"best practice" is, in his experience, often wrong or beside the point.

## What the piece previews

It functions as a table of contents for a planned series, flagging topics
including bad advice, managing emotional state while coding, working
faster, and testing practice. It explicitly scopes out large/long-lived
codebases and team collaboration as topics it will not cover.
