# Mathematics in the age of AI

Author: Terence Tao. Based on a public lecture at the 2026 International
Congress of Mathematicians. The essay conditions on the hypothesis that
AI systems capable of research-level mathematical tasks arrive, and asks what
the mathematics community's actual goals and values are, using
problem-solving as its worked case study — deliberately *not* asking whether
AI will get there, but what changes if it does.

## The five-stage pipeline

Tao decomposes mathematical problem-solving into five phases — generation,
verification, exposition, publication/acceptance, and canonicalization — and
argues AI is positioned to accelerate the early stages (generating candidate
proofs, mechanically verifying them) while the later stages (exposition,
acceptance, canonicalization into the field's shared body of knowledge) stay
fundamentally human-centered, because they are social and institutional
processes, not just correctness checks.

## From scarcity to abundance

Mathematics has historically operated under scarcity — the hard part is
finding a proof at all. Tao argues AI shifts the bottleneck to **abundance**:
a potentially overwhelming volume of machine-generated proofs, most never
worth a human's attention, creating new bottlenecks in verification and
comprehension rather than solving them away.

## The human-responsibility standard

The essay's central normative claim: a proof should be considered incomplete
unless a human expert can adequately explain it. Human understanding is
treated as a requirement of a completed proof, not an optional byproduct of
having one.

## Recommendations

Tao calls for transparency about AI tool use in mathematical work, greater
institutional valuation of review/refereeing labor (which does not scale the
way generation does), new digital infrastructure for the resulting volume,
and explicit human-understandability criteria in journal standards and
hiring decisions.
