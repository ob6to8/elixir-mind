# A fortuitous decade as an indie software developer

Jeff Johnson quit his job on August 9, 2016 to become an indie Mac/iOS
developer without a specific product plan. His original project,
[Underpass](/survey/bookmarks.md) (2017), an encrypted peer-to-peer
messaging and file-transfer app, was his genuine passion project but a
commercial failure — under $3,000 in lifetime revenue before it was pulled
from the App Store in 2021. For roughly five years he depleted savings while
shipping apps that didn't gain traction, and attributes part of the struggle
to age-based hiring bias in tech: an experienced developer with "outmoded"
skills (AppKit, Objective-C) and no CS degree found it hard to get hired
back into employment even as a fallback.

## The turning point

The turning point was September 20, 2021 — by his account, one month before
he'd have faced bankruptcy — when Apple shipped iOS 15 with Safari extension
support. **StopTheMadness**, a content-blocking Safari extension he'd built
in 2018 (inspired by someone else's tweet, not his original vision),
suddenly had a much larger addressable platform. It went on to generate over
93% of his lifetime App Store revenue, later expanding to mobile and a paid
Pro tier (2023).

## Reading

Johnson is explicit that this is a story about luck and survivorship bias,
not a reproducible strategy: his business philosophy is "surfing the waves"
rather than long-term planning, and he deliberately stayed exclusively on
the App Store (despite criticizing Apple's platform policies) partly as a
hedge — so he'd have a résumé to show if the indie venture failed outright.
